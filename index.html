About

Clinician-scientist

Dr Divya Nagabushana is an Endowed Chair Assistant Professor of Neurology at the Indian Institute of Science Medical School, Bengaluru, and a consultant neurologist and epileptologist. Her clinical and research interests span adult and pediatric epilepsy, electroencephalography, presurgical evaluation, stereo-EEG, genetics, neuromodulation, and mechanisms of epileptogenesis.

She completed a postdoctoral clinical fellowship in epilepsy at the University of Illinois Chicago (2023–2024), following DM Neurology and MD Pediatrics training at Bangalore Medical College and Research Institute. She holds American Board of Clinical Neurophysiology certification in clinical neurophysiology and epilepsy monitoring.

Her research program aims to translate quantitative EEG and electrophysiological biomarkers into clinically meaningful tools, while studying how early-life brain injury leads to chronic epilepsy.

Research laboratory

Translational Epilepsy & Neurophysiology Research

An emerging research program at IISc connecting signal-level discoveries to clinical decision-making and disease mechanisms. The areas below represent active work and planned directions; laboratory infrastructure and cohorts are being developed.

01 · Neurophysiology

EEG biomarkers and automated detection

Interictal epileptiform discharges, seizure detection and classification, spectral and aperiodic features, connectivity and interpretable quantitative EEG.

02 · Epilepsy surgery

Seizure onset zone localization

Intracranial EEG and SEEG analytics, electrical source localization and electrophysiological markers to improve presurgical evaluation.

03 · Experimental neuroscience

Epileptogenesis after neonatal injury

Developing a rodent model of neonatal hypoglycemic brain injury to investigate subsequent epilepsy and longitudinal video-EEG changes.

04 · Clinical epidemiology

EEG yield and critical-care EEG

Extended outpatient EEG diagnostic yield and lateralized periodic discharges in critically ill patients, including seizure associations and outcomes.

05 · Research infrastructure

Epilepsy registry, repository and biobank

Planned deeply phenotyped longitudinal clinical datasets, curated EEG recordings and biological samples for translational studies.

06 · Precision epilepsy

Genomics and metabolomics

Longer-term integration of molecular profiling with clinical phenotypes and EEG biomarkers to explore disease mechanisms.
